Why it is worth surfacing

A text report buries it among 448 lines. In the view, a 24-caller node with no edge to the backend side is immediately obvious.

Four decisions in this layout

  • Never draw the whole map. 448 endpoints × 99 screens is where a DOM graph dies — exactly why jsoncrack needs a NODE_LIMIT. Pick a node, expand one degree; to go deeper, make another node the centre.
  • Confidence is a first-class citizen, not a footnote. Filterable in the top bar, badged on every edge. Otherwise inferred gets read as fact.
  • Always a file:line and a hop count. An answer has to be verifiable by hand in 30 seconds, otherwise it is just an assertion.
  • What is unknown has to show, never hide: “0 fields measured”, “33 calls stop at the module”, “252 endpoints nobody calls”.

Three questions that need a decision

  • 1. Should the graph centre on an endpoint (as in the mockup) or on a screen? An endpoint fits “I am about to change this API”; a screen fits “what does this screen consume”. Both are possible, but one has to be the default.
  • 2. Should a field be a node in the graph, or stay in the right panel as it is now? As nodes, the screen → field path becomes visible, but 2,826 fields would flood the graph.
  • 3. Should the three alerts (endpoints nobody calls · fields declared but never sent · calls that stop at the module) be a tab of their own, or just colouring in the list? They are what people open the tool to find, but they also make noise easily.
